**Figure 1.** Threshold regression of log1p(Appliances) on the three sensors with the smallest Holm-adjusted p-values (ties broken by the sup-F statistic). Points are mean log energy within 40 quantile bins; the red line is the fitted threshold model; the shaded band is the 95% block-bootstrap CI of the breakpoint. The panel title states whether the threshold met all five support criteria (Holm-significant, positive slope, interior breakpoint, ≥5% data past threshold, and significant after hour-of-day control). **Main result (Q2): 0 of 36 sensor–direction pairs met all five criteria.** The hour-of-day control is decisive: all 6 pairs that passed criteria 1–4 became non-significant once hour was controlled. The test can only detect kinks of roughly 56% energy change (range 15–92%); absence of supported thresholds does not rule out smaller kinks. Associations only; crossing-frequency columns in threshold_table.csv are empty because they are populated only for supported thresholds — the empty table is the correct output for a null result.

**Figure 2.** Out-of-sample R² (log1p scale) for each feature group and model on identical contiguous time blocks with a 1-day buffer. Bars are means across blocks; whiskers show the range; the grey band spans the R² reached by rv1 and the 20 random noise columns (the null floor). **Main result (Q3): temporal features clearly outperform environmental features under strict time-blocking (tree models: R² ≈ 0.243–0.248 vs. noise floor). The finding is model-dependent: SVR shows a small positive incremental contribution from environment (+0.070), while tree models do not.** A simple hour-of-day mean baseline (R² ≈ 0.241) matches the three tree models on temporal features, and a Ridge regression on time + raw sensors reaches R² ≈ 0.302 — both computed on the same test blocks and interpreted as exploratory. XGBoost underperformed and showed high instability across environments (observed range: −0.458 to −0.335 for the indoor group); results should be interpreted cautiously. No champion model is named: fold-to-fold variance is large, paired differences between models are small, and simple baselines are competitive. Values express incremental predictive value, not causal or uniquely attributable variance.

**Q1 note.** Band ratio analysis found 17 of 72 sensor-band comparisons with block-bootstrap intervals excluding 1.0. No multiple-comparison correction was applied (approximately 4 false positives expected at α = 0.05). These associations are not controlled for time-of-day or season, so environmental effects may reflect confounding by daily or seasonal routines. Descriptive evidence only.
